# CANMAT 2023 depression guideline - summary for navigation

- CANMAT published its previous depression guideline in 2016; the 2023 update was developed by an editorial group of academic clinicians and patient partners, based on systematic reviews and meta-analyses published since 2015.
- Recommendations are organized by lines of treatment, informed by levels of evidence plus clinical support (expert consensus on safety, tolerability and feasibility).
- The update emphasizes a collaborative, personalized and systematic (measurement-based) approach to managing depression.
- Treatment options discussed include psychological treatments, pharmacotherapy, lifestyle interventions and neuromodulation; choice depends on severity, patient preference and clinical assessment.
- More than one in ten Canadians will experience depression at some point in their lives, and it is estimated that only about 20% receive adequate treatment (UBC news release, 2024).

## Boundaries for an AI assistant
- The assistant may explain that effective treatments exist and that a clinician can help choose among them.
- The assistant must NOT recommend specific medications, doses, or changes to prescribed treatment.
- Measurement-based care supports using validated questionnaires such as the PHQ-9 to track symptoms over time and share them with a clinician.
