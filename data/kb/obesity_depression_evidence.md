# Obesity and depression are linked in both directions

- A meta-review of systematic reviews found a statistically significant association between obesity and depression in all nine included meta-analyses, with odds ratios from 1.18 to 1.57 (BJPsych Open).
- The association appears reciprocal and may be modified by age and sex. A 2010 meta-analysis of over 58,000 participants established a bidirectional association among adults aged 20 to 59; the association was not statistically significant under age 20 (as summarized by Friedman et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2025).
- Proposed mechanisms include HPA-axis (stress hormone) dysregulation, inflammation, weight stigma, body image, sleep and activity changes.
- A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of Mendelian randomization studies (Obesity Reviews, doi:10.1111/obr.70156) reported evidence that general adiposity is causally associated with depression.

## Why this matters for one combined tool
Because the two conditions interact, a navigator that handles mood screening and weight/nutrition guidance together can surface links a single-topic app would miss (for example low mood reducing activity, or weight stigma worsening mood). Recommendations should be health-focused and non-stigmatizing, in line with Obesity Canada guidance.
