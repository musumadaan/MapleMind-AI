# Measured obesity in Canadian adults

Statistics Canada reported measured (not self-reported) results from the Canadian Health Measures Survey (CHMS) for 2022 to 2024.

## Key findings
- Among adults aged 18 to 79, measured obesity prevalence rose from 25% in 2016-2019 (pre-pandemic) to 33% in 2022-2024.
- Prevalence was highest among adults aged 40 to 59 (38% of males, 35% of females). Among adults 60 to 79 it was 35% of males and 33% of females.
- The largest increase was among young adults aged 18 to 39, starting from 22% before the pandemic.
- Statistics Canada notes that pandemic-related lifestyle changes such as reduced physical activity and more sedentary time may have contributed.

## Measurement caveats
- Self-reported BMI tends to be underestimated compared with direct measurement, so survey estimates based on self-report are lower than CHMS measured estimates.
- Waist circumference can reveal weight-related health risk not reflected by BMI; abdominal obesity thresholds follow Health Canada guidelines.

## Longer-term trend
- A 2026 Statistics Canada infographic states that in 2024 about 1 in 3 adults had obesity versus about 1 in 5 adults in 2000, and that the odds of having a chronic condition increase the longer someone has obesity.
