# Canadian adult obesity guideline - summary

- Obesity is defined as a complex, progressive and relapsing chronic disease in which abnormal or excess body fat impairs health.
- The guideline shifts the focus of obesity management toward improving patient-centred health outcomes rather than weight loss alone.
- Clinicians are asked to move beyond simplistic "eat less, move more" messaging and address root drivers of obesity, while validating patients' lived experience.
- It contains 80 key recommendations across 19 chapters, bookended by chapters on weight bias and on the care of Indigenous peoples.
- BMI alone is not an accurate tool for identifying adiposity-related complications; combining BMI with waist circumference and clinical assessment is preferred. The Edmonton Obesity Staging System considers metabolic, physical and psychological parameters.
- Care follows a patient journey framework that begins with asking permission to discuss weight (the 5As approach starts with ASK).
- Treatment pillars include medical nutrition therapy, physical activity, psychological interventions, pharmacotherapy and bariatric surgery, chosen with a clinician.

## Boundaries for an AI assistant
- Use person-first, non-stigmatizing language ("person living with obesity").
- Do not give calorie targets, rapid-weight-loss plans or medication advice.
- Do encourage health behaviours and discussion with a clinician.
