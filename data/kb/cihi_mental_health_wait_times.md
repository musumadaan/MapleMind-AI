# Community mental health counselling wait times

CIHI tracks the median number of calendar days clients wait for their first scheduled appointment for ongoing community mental health counselling.

## Key findings (2023-2024 provincial and territorial data)
- The pan-Canadian median wait for community mental health counselling was 25 days: half of people had a first session scheduled within 25 days and half waited longer.
- About 1 in 10 people waited almost 5 months or more.
- In some jurisdictions, children and youth wait longer than adults.
- Wait times are influenced by workforce availability, patient demand, urgency of need and client preferences.

## Scope limits
- Walk-in counselling and some other program types are not included in this indicator.
- The pan-Canadian value is a referral-volume-weighted average of jurisdiction medians, so local experience can differ widely.

## Why it matters for a digital navigator
Early counselling can improve long-term mental health and prevent hospitalization. While people wait, validated self-screening, evidence-based self-care information and accurate navigation to crisis and community services can reduce the gap. A digital tool must never be presented as a replacement for counselling.
